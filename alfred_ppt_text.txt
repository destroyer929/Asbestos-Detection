==================== PAGE 2 ====================
The Challenge: Asbestos Inspection in Historical Blueprints
▶Strict Japanese Regulatory Mandate:
Japan’s Air Pollution Control Act mandates identifying all asbestos materials prior to
building demolition; missing friable spray asbestos carries severe criminal and legal liability.
▶Severe Scanning & Photocopy Artifacts:
Historical Showa-era blueprints (1970s–1980s) suffer from carbon smudging, photocopy
fade, paper yellowing, micro-tilts, and low scanning resolution.
▶80%+ Structural Dimension Clutter:
Blueprints are saturated with structural measurements (4,500, FL±0, CH=2700,
H-300x150) that corrupt off-the-shelf OCR engines.
▶Commercial Trade Brand Jargon:
Blueprints specify vintage manufacturer trade brands (e.g., Tomlex, Dailotone, Tonlight)
rather than generic legal terms.

Stage 2: Adaptive Pre-Flight Quality Brain
▶Sub-15ms Physical Noise Quantification:
Evaluates raw image noise standard deviation σ (Immerkær metric), P95 histogram
brightness, and Laplacian sharpness before applying any transformations.
▶Level 0 Clean Passthrough (CAD Stroke Preservation):
High-resolution digital scans (σ < 4.0) bypass destructive binarization entirely, preserving
delicate Kanji strokes and fine 0.1mm architectural line weights.
▶Level 1 Deskew & CLAHE Contrast Normalization:
Mild scans undergo Radon-transform deskew (±5◦) and tile-based CLAHE contrast
adaptation to recover faded ink without threshold distortion.
▶Level 2 Sauvola Local Binarization:
Heavily degraded Showa-era prints undergo adaptive windowed binarization with
background illumination leveling to remove deep carbon smudging and paper yellowing.

==================== PAGE 8 ====================
Stage 4: 5-Tier Hierarchical Matching Engine
▶Unicode NFKC & SudachiPy Morphological Tokenization:
Normalizes full-width alphanumeric tokens (2 5 m m →25mm) and executes Mode-B
compound word splitting to isolate core construction nouns.
▶Sub-Millisecond Hash & Domain Regex Tiers:
Tier 1 performs O(1) exact lookup (< 0.1ms) across 27,066 keys; Tier 2 evaluates
precompiled Japanese domain rules for compound syntax (e.g., spray + rockwool).
▶C++ SIMD Fuzzy & Sub-Character N-Gram Approximation:
Tier 3 deploys RapidFuzz SIMD Levenshtein distance (≥78%) to survive OCR typos; Tier
4 applies character 2/3-gram Dice overlap (≥65%) for partial strings.
▶Tier 5 Local LLM Arbitrator (Qwen3:1.7B):
Unmatched candidate tokens route to a localized Ollama LLM, performing offline
semantic regulatory triage into Positive, Certified Safe, or Human Review.

Regulatory Knowledge Base: MLIT & JATI Standards
▶Official Japanese Statutory Database:
Built directly upon Japan’s MLIT (Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and
Tourism) and JATI asbestos registries, containing 3,425 materials and 27,066 lookup
aliases.
▶Statutory 3-Level Hazard Stratification:
Rigorously classifies materials into Level 1 (friable spray asbestos, severe risk), Level 2
(thermal pipe insulation), and Level 3 (non-friable molded slate/vinyl tiles).
▶Certified Non-Asbestos Benchmark Controls:
Explicitly catalogues safe standard building elements (gypsum board, rockwool post-2004,
glass wool) to prevent false positives and unnecessary client abatement costs.
▶Automated Regulatory Citation Generation:
Links detected hazardous items directly to Japanese Air Pollution Control Act statutory
clauses and inspection manuals for legal report generation.

==================== PAGE 12 ====================
System & Operational Limitations
▶Faded historical Showa-era Diazo prints (aoyaki) and handwritten cursive Kanji
annotations exhibit higher OCR transcription ambiguity.
▶Classical morphological table parsing (cv2.MORPH_RECT) requires continuous
orthogonal lines and struggles on borderless schedules.
▶Identical trade brand names spanning pre- and post-asbestos bans require the
building’s completion year for conclusive diagnosis.
▶Blueprints reflect original design specifications but cannot account for
undocumented on-site contractor substitutions or later renovations.
▶Tier-5 local LLM arbitration (Qwen3:1.7B) introduces a 1–2 second inference
delay on edge hardware without dedicated GPU acceleration.
